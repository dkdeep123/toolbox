Comprehensive Guide to Indian GST: Forward & Reverse Formulas

By ToolBoxPro Editorial Team • Published: November 2026 • 6 Min Read

The implementation of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) transformed India's indirect taxation framework into a unified, destination-based multi-stage tax system. For business owners, freelance service providers, and consumers alike, understanding how GST is computed on both a forward (exclusive) and reverse (inclusive) basis is essential for accurate invoicing and expense auditing.

A frequent point of confusion is determining the base taxable amount from a gross, tax-inclusive receipt. This educational guide breaks down the core GST mathematical formulas, the structural division between CGST, SGST, and IGST, tax invoice calculations, and how Input Tax Credit (ITC) prevents tax cascading.

1. The Four Pillars of Indian GST: CGST, SGST, IGST & UTGST

Depending on whether a commercial transaction occurs within a single state (Intra-State) or across state borders (Inter-State), GST is apportioned differently:

  • Intra-State Supply (Within Same State): The tax is split equally between the Central and State Governments. If an 18% GST rate applies, the invoice bills 9% Central GST (CGST) and 9% State GST (SGST).
  • Union Territory Supply (Within UT without Legislature): Charged as equal parts CGST + UTGST (e.g. Chandigarh, Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar).
  • Inter-State Supply (Between Different States): The full tax rate is collected as Integrated GST (IGST) by the Central Government, which subsequently apportions the destination state's share. For example, a supplier in Maharashtra shipping goods to a buyer in Karnataka bills 18% IGST.

2. Forward GST Calculation: Adding GST to a Net Base Amount

When you know the pre-tax net cost of a product or service and need to add GST, the forward calculation applies:

GST Amount = (Net Base Amount × GST Rate) / 100
Gross Total = Net Base Amount + GST Amount

Worked Example: A software consulting firm bills a client for ₹50,000 net services subject to 18% GST:

  • GST Amount = (50,000 × 18) / 100 = ₹9,000
  • For Intra-state: CGST (9%) = ₹4,500 and SGST (9%) = ₹4,500
  • Total Invoice Value = ₹50,000 + ₹9,000 = ₹59,000

3. Reverse GST Calculation: Extracting GST from a Gross Inclusive Price

A common mistake made by consumers and accountants is simply subtracting the GST percentage from the final gross bill (e.g., calculating 18% of ₹118 and subtracting it yields ₹21.24, which is mathematically incorrect!).

Because the tax was calculated on the original base, extracting the tax requires the true reverse algebraic formula:

Net Base Amount = (Gross Inclusive Price × 100) / (100 + GST Rate)
GST Component = Gross Inclusive Price - Net Base Amount

Worked Example: You purchase an electronic appliance for ₹23,600 inclusive of 18% GST:

  • Net Base Amount = (23,600 × 100) / (100 + 18) = 23,60,000 / 118 = ₹20,000
  • GST Amount = ₹23,600 - ₹20,000 = ₹3,600
  • Verification: 18% of ₹20,000 is exactly ₹3,600!

5. How Input Tax Credit (ITC) Eliminates Tax Cascading

The defining innovation of GST is the Input Tax Credit (ITC) mechanism. Prior to GST, taxes paid on raw materials (like excise and state VAT) could not be offset against service taxes, causing "tax on tax" (cascading). Under GST:

  • A manufacturer pays ₹1,800 GST when buying raw materials (Input Tax).
  • Upon selling the finished product, the manufacturer charges the customer ₹2,500 GST (Output Tax).
  • Instead of remitting ₹2,500, the manufacturer offsets the ₹1,800 already paid and remits only the net difference of ₹700 to the government.
