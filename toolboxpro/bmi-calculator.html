Understanding Body Mass Index (BMI)

Body Mass Index is a widely used screening tool developed by the World Health Organization (WHO) to gauge body fat based on height and weight. While not measuring body fat directly, research demonstrates BMI correlates well with direct body composition assessments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the official WHO BMI categories?
Underweight is less than 18.5; Normal weight is 18.5 to 24.9; Overweight is 25 to 29.9; and Obesity is a BMI of 30 or greater.
Does BMI distinguish between muscle mass and fat?
No. Athletes or bodybuilders with high muscle mass may have a high BMI without excess body fat. BMI is a general screening indicator rather than a clinical diagnostic measure.
What is the mathematical BMI formula?
In metric units: BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]². In imperial: BMI = 703 × Weight (lbs) / [Height (inches)]².
