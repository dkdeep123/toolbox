The Complete Guide to Indian Govt Exam Photo & Document Specifications

By ToolBoxPro Editorial Team • Published: November 2026 • 8 Min Read

Every year, over 25 million candidates apply for competitive entrance and recruitment examinations across India, including NTA NEET, JEE Main, SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS, UPSC Civil Services, and Railway Recruitment Boards (RRB). Shockingly, administrative data indicates that between 3% and 7% of all applications face rejection or provisional withholding solely due to incorrectly formatted passport photographs, illegible signatures, or oversized certificate PDFs.

Online application portals use automated computer-vision scripts and rigid server upload limits. If your photograph is 201 KB on a portal that caps uploads at 200 KB, or if your signature dimensions fail the mandatory 2:1 aspect ratio, the upload gateway will reject the file instantly. This comprehensive reference guide compiles the official 2026 specifications across all premier examination authorities.

2. Deep Dive: Passport Photograph Regulations

Modern recruitment portals employ artificial intelligence verification models to detect facial symmetry and contrast. The following rules are universally enforced:

  • 80% Face Coverage: The frame must be tightly composed from the top of your hair to your collarbone. Full-body or waist-up photos will be rejected. Both ears must be clearly visible.
  • Background Color: The background must be pure white or very light grey. Do NOT use colored backdrops, outdoor foliage, or domestic room settings.
  • No Eyeglasses or Headgear: Unless worn for strict religious mandates, hats, caps, turbans obscuring the forehead, and tinted spectacles are forbidden. Clear prescription spectacles are permissible only if there is zero flash reflection on the lenses.
  • Neutral Facial Expression: Both eyes must be open, looking directly into the camera lens with mouth closed. Smiling broadly or tilting the head will fail verification.
  • Name & Date Imprint (NEET / UPSC): NEET UG bulletins require the candidate's name and the date of taking the photograph to be clearly printed at the bottom of the photo. UPSC requires the photograph to have been captured within 10 days of submitting Part 1 registration.

3. Signature Formatting: Avoid the "All-Caps" Disqualification Trap

Signature rejection is the single most common clerical reason for candidate disqualification. Keep these rules front and center:

  • Never Sign in Capital Letters: Signing in ALL CAPITAL BLOCK LETTERS is treated as an invalid signature under Indian contract law and examination guidelines. Use your natural, continuous cursive handwriting.
  • Paper & Ink Specifications: Sign inside a 3.5 × 1.5 cm box drawn in light pencil on unruled, clean 80 GSM white paper. Use a dark black or blue ballpoint pen. Avoid gel pens or fountain pens, which bleed ink into the paper grain when scanned.
  • Crop Borders Tight: When photographing your signature on a smartphone, crop closely around the ink strokes. Do not include large white borders or shadows from your phone.

4. Category & Marksheet Certificate Uploads (PDF Standards)

For EWS, OBC-NCL, SC/ST, and PwD certificates, as well as 10th and 12th passing marksheets, portal rules mandate PDF format:

  • Strict KB Ceilings: Portals such as NTA enforce an upper limit of 300 KB. RRB and GATE accept up to 500 KB.
  • Legibility of Official Seals: When compressing a scanned PDF, avoid over-compression that blurs the Tehsildar/District Magistrate's stamp, digital signature barcode, or document serial number.
