Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mathematical compound interest formula?
The mathematical formula is: A = P × (1 + r / n)^(n × t), where:
  • A = Final maturity amount
  • P = Principal investment
  • r = Annual interest rate in decimal form (Rate / 100)
  • n = Compounding frequency per year (1 for annual, 4 for quarterly, 12 for monthly, 365 for daily)
  • t = Investment duration in years
How frequently do Indian banks compound interest on Fixed Deposits?
In India, commercial banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, PNB) and the Public Provident Fund (PPF) compound interest quarterly (every 3 months). That means your annual yield is always slightly higher than the stated annual interest rate.
What is the difference between Simple Interest and Compound Interest?
In Simple Interest, returns are calculated solely on the original principal throughout the entire tenure. In Compound Interest, returns from each period are added to the principal, generating compound interest on interest. Over a 10 to 20-year horizon, compound interest can deliver 50% to 200%+ more total wealth than simple interest.
What is the Rule of 72?
The Rule of 72 is an easy formula to find how fast your investment doubles: Years to Double = 72 / Expected Annual Return Rate. For example, at an 8% return, money doubles in 72 / 8 = 9 years. At 12%, money doubles in just 6 years!
