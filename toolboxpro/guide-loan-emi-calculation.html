How Banks Calculate Loan EMI: Reducing Balance vs Flat Rate

By ToolBoxPro Editorial Team • Published: November 2026 • 6 Min Read

Whether you are borrowing for a home purchase, acquiring an automobile, or securing a personal education loan, the Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is the core figure dictating your monthly cash flow. An EMI represents a fixed amount paid by a borrower to a financial lender on a set date each calendar month until the total principal loan and accrued interest are fully settled.

However, many borrowers are unaware that their monthly payment is not split equally between principal and interest throughout the tenure. In early years, up to 70% of each EMI payment goes purely toward interest, leaving the outstanding principal barely touched. This in-depth banking guide demystifies the reducing-balance amortization formula, compares it with deceptive flat-rate offers, and reveals proven prepayment strategies to save lakhs in interest.

1. The Standard Reducing-Balance EMI Formula

In accordance with Reserve Bank of India (RBI) lending guidelines and international banking standards, commercial banks calculate monthly installments using the standard amortization equation:

E = P × r × [ (1 + r)^n / ( (1 + r)^n - 1 ) ]

Where the components are defined as:

  • E: Equated Monthly Installment (the monthly payment amount).
  • P: Principal loan borrowed (e.g. ₹10,00,000 for a car or home down payment).
  • r: Monthly periodic interest rate, calculated as Annual Interest Rate / (12 × 100). For an 8.5% annual rate, r = 8.5 / 1200 = 0.0070833.
  • n: Total loan tenure in months, calculated as Years × 12 (e.g., a 5-year loan has n = 60 months; a 20-year home loan has n = 240 months).

2. Worked Example: ₹10,00,000 Loan at 8.5% for 5 Years

Let's run through the calculation with actual numbers:

  • Principal (P): ₹10,00,000
  • Annual Rate (R): 8.50% → r = 0.0070833
  • Tenure (n): 5 years → 60 months

Plugging these values into the formula:

(1 + r)^60 = (1.0070833)^60 ≈ 1.5273
E = 10,00,000 × 0.0070833 × [ 1.5273 / (1.5273 - 1) ]
E = 7,083.33 × 2.8964 ≈ ₹20,517 per month

Over 60 months, the borrower pays: 60 × ₹20,516.53 = ₹12,30,992. The total interest paid over 5 years is ₹2,30,992.

3. The Amortization Decomposition: Principal vs Interest

Even though the EMI remains constant at ₹20,517 every single month, the internal split between principal and interest shifts with each passing installment. As the outstanding loan balance drops, the interest charged for that month decreases, allowing a larger portion of your EMI to erode the principal:

4. The "Flat Rate" vs "Reducing Balance" Trap

Non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and vehicle dealers frequently quote loans using a Flat Interest Rate (e.g., "Special Car Loan at only 7.5% flat rate!"). This is often misleading:

  • Flat Rate Method: Interest is calculated on the original full principal for the entire loan duration, completely ignoring the fact that you are repaying principal every month. For a ₹5,00,000 loan at 7.5% flat for 5 years, annual interest is ₹37,500. Total interest = 5 × ₹37,500 = ₹1,87,500.
  • Reducing Balance Equivalent: In a reducing-balance loan at 7.5%, total interest would only be ₹1,01,400.
  • The Reality: A 7.5% flat interest rate is equivalent to an alarming 13.8% reducing balance interest rate! Always ask the lender for the reducing-balance Annual Percentage Rate (APR) before signing.

5. How Prepayment Saves Massive Interest (The 1-Extra-EMI Strategy)

Because interest is calculated on outstanding principal, every rupee paid above the standard EMI goes 100% directly toward principal reduction. On a ₹50,00,000 home loan at 8.5% for 20 years:

  • Standard tenure: Total interest paid = ₹54,13,879 (more than the original loan amount!).
  • The 1-Extra-EMI Strategy: Paying just ONE extra EMI payment every year (13 payments instead of 12) reduces your effective loan tenure from 20 years down to 16 years and 2 months, saving over ₹12.5 Lakhs in interest!
